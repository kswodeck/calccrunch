## When Deals Actually Start in 2026

The days of a single doorbuster morning are long gone. Retailers now stagger "early access" sales across several weeks before the official date:

| Retailer | Early Access Starts |
|---|---|
| Walmart | October 28 |
| Target | November 3 |
| Best Buy | November 7 |
| Home Depot | November 10 |
| Costco | November 12 |
| All retailers (peak week) | Week of November 15 |
| Black Friday | November 27 |
| Cyber Monday | November 30 |

The practical takeaway: if you're hunting for a specific big-ticket item, start watching prices in late October, but don't expect the *deepest* discounts until Black Friday/Cyber Monday week itself.

## What to Buy When

Not every category discounts on the same schedule. Based on how retailers have historically run these sales:

**Buy during Black Friday/Cyber Monday week (Nov 23-30):**
- TVs, laptops, and other electronics
- Headphones and smart home devices
- Small kitchen appliances
- Toys and games

**Wait even longer if you can:**
- Brand-new flagship phones and the newest models of anything (deeper cuts tend to come later in the product cycle, not during the first holiday season)
- Winter coats, bedding, and fitness gear — these categories often see their steepest markdowns closer to January

**Don't bother shopping early for a "deal" on:**
- Items only available through October promotions (like Prime-style October sales events) — in past years, TV discounts during these October events have run 5-15 percentage points shallower than what the same models hit during Black Friday week. Only buy in October if the price already matches the Black Friday level you're targeting.

## Building a Shopping List That Doesn't Wreck Your Budget

Going in with a plan beats reacting to "limited time" urgency every time. A simple three-step approach:

1. **Set a total holiday spending number first.** Use the [holiday gift budget calculator](/calculators/holiday-gift-budget-calculator) to allocate your total budget across the people on your list *before* a single sale starts — it's much easier to say no to an impulse buy when you already know how much room you have left.
2. **Prioritize scarce items first.** High-demand, low-stock items (new gaming consoles, popular toys, limited-run electronics) sell out fastest — if something on your list falls into this category, plan to buy it the moment doorbuster pricing goes live rather than waiting to comparison shop.
3. **Recheck your running total against the [budget calculator](/calculators/budget-calculator)** after a few purchases. It's easy to lose track of how much you've spent across a dozen different sales emails and browser tabs — a mid-shopping-season check-in catches overspending before it becomes a problem in December.

## A Few Rules Worth Following

- **Compare total cost, not just percentage off.** A 40%-off item with high shipping can cost more than a 25%-off item with free shipping.
- **Check the return window before buying**, especially for electronics — Black Friday purchases sometimes carry extended return periods, but not always.
- **Don't rush everything into one day.** Many retailers extend Black Friday pricing through the weekend and roll in new Cyber Monday deals, so if you miss a specific item on Friday, Saturday and Sunday are worth checking before you assume you lost the deal.
- **Activate loyalty programs and price-tracking tools before the sales start**, not during — trying to sign up for a store card or browser extension mid-checkout wastes time you don't have on a fast-moving doorbuster deal.
