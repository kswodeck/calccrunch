Daylight saving time ends on **Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 a.m. local time**. At that moment, clocks in most of the U.S. and Canada fall back one hour to 1:00 a.m., giving most people an extra hour of sleep and shifting sunrise and sunset about an hour earlier for the next several months. Below you'll find exactly what changes that weekend, who's exempt, how it affects scheduling across time zones, and when clocks spring forward again.

## Key 2026-2027 Clock Change Dates

| Event | Date | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| DST ends ("fall back") | Sunday, Nov 1, 2026, 2:00 a.m. | Clocks move back 1 hour to 1:00 a.m.; standard time begins |

| DST begins ("spring forward") | Sunday, Mar 14, 2027, 2:00 a.m. | Clocks move forward 1 hour to 3:00 a.m.; daylight time resumes |

## What Actually Happens on November 1, 2026

At 2:00 a.m. local time, clocks are set back to 1:00 a.m., so that hour between 1:00 and 2:00 a.m. technically happens twice. In practice, most people experience this as simply waking up to a clock that's an hour "earlier" than expected — phones, computers, and most modern appliances update automatically, but analog clocks, ovens, and older car dashboards typically don't.

The bigger everyday effect isn't the one-time change itself but the weeks that follow: sunrise and sunset both shift about an hour earlier, so afternoons get darker noticeably faster, especially in northern latitudes. Many people also notice a few days of mild sleep disruption, similar to a small dose of jet lag, since the change affects your body's exposure to morning and evening light.

## Who Doesn't Change Their Clocks

Not everyone in the U.S. observes daylight saving time. Most of **Arizona** (outside the Navajo Nation, which does observe DST) and all of **Hawaii** stay on standard time year-round and skip the change entirely. Several U.S. territories, including Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, and Guam, also don't observe DST. If you regularly coordinate with contacts in these places, remember that your time difference with them will shift by an hour on November 1 even though their clocks don't move.

## Why the Time Change Trips Up International Scheduling

Because the U.S. and Europe don't switch on the same weekend, there's a temporary window each year where the usual time difference between them is off by an hour. In 2026, Europe falls back on **October 25**, a full week before the U.S. does on **November 1**. During that week, anyone using a hardcoded time difference (rather than a live converter) for a recurring meeting will be an hour off in one direction or the other.

## Preparing for the Change

A few practical steps make the transition smoother:

- **Confirm which of your devices auto-update.** Smartphones, laptops, and most smart home devices adjust automatically; ovens, microwaves, thermostats, and analog clocks usually need a manual reset.
- **Double-check recurring calendar invites** that span time zones — especially any set up before this year, since some calendar tools handle the "gap week" between U.S. and European transitions differently.
- **Adjust bedtime gradually** in the days leading up to November 1 if you're sensitive to the shift — 10-15 minutes earlier per night can soften the adjustment.
- **Plan for earlier darkness** in your evening routine, especially for outdoor activities, commutes, or picking up kids from after-school programs.

## Is Daylight Saving Time Going Away?

There's ongoing legislative interest in ending the twice-yearly clock change. The U.S. House of Representatives passed the Sunshine Protection Act in July 2026, which would make daylight saving time permanent nationwide, and the bill was sent to the Senate. As of this writing, it has not passed the Senate or become law, so the existing schedule — falling back November 1, 2026 and springing forward March 14, 2027 — remains in effect for now. Any change would require further Congressional action, so it's worth checking current news before assuming the clock change is going away this year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I gain or lose an hour of sleep in November?**
You gain an hour. "Fall back" means clocks move backward, so the hour between 1:00 and 2:00 a.m. on November 1, 2026 effectively happens twice, giving most people an extra hour compared to a normal night.

**Does daylight saving time end at the same time everywhere in the U.S.?**
The 2:00 a.m. changeover happens according to each location's own local time, so it occurs at the same "clock moment" everywhere that observes it — but Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) and Hawaii don't observe DST at all, so their clocks don't change.

**Will my phone automatically adjust for daylight saving time?**
Yes, for the vast majority of smartphones, computers, and internet-connected devices, as long as their time zone and automatic-time settings are enabled. Manual devices like wall clocks, car dashboards, and some appliances need to be changed by hand.

**Why do the U.S. and Europe change their clocks on different dates?**
The two regions set their transition dates independently through separate legislation (the EU sets its dates by directive; the U.S. by federal law), and neither is required to coordinate with the other. This creates a short window each spring and fall where the usual time difference between U.S. and European cities shifts by an hour.

**When does daylight saving time start again after November 2026?**
Clocks spring forward again on **Sunday, March 14, 2027**, at 2:00 a.m. local time, moving forward to 3:00 a.m. and resuming daylight saving time through the following November.
