The IRS has officially set the 2027 401(k) contribution limit at **$25,500**, up $1,000 from the 2026 limit of $24,500 — the second consecutive $1,000 increase. The standard catch-up contribution for savers 50 and older rises to **$8,500**, and the special "super catch-up" for ages 60–63 climbs to **$11,750**, bringing the maximum possible 2027 contribution for that age group to **$37,250**. These figures come from Notice 2026-66, announced November 12, 2026 (IR-2026-136), and take effect for the 2027 plan year starting January 2027.

If you're setting next year's payroll deductions now, here's exactly what changed and how to use the extra room.

## What Changed for 2027

| | 2026 | 2027 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 401(k)/403(b)/457(b)/TSP elective deferral limit | $24,500 | $25,500 | +$1,000 |
| Catch-up contribution (age 50+) | $8,000 | $8,500 | +$500 |
| "Super" catch-up (ages 60–63) | $11,250 | $11,750 | +$500 |
| Max total contribution, ages 60–63 | $35,750 | $37,250 | +$1,500 |
| Traditional/Roth IRA limit | $7,500 | $7,500 | No change |
| IRA catch-up (age 50+) | $1,100 | $1,100 | No change |
| SIMPLE plan limit | $17,000 | $17,500 | +$500 |
| IRA deduction phase-out (single/HOH) | $153,000–$168,000 | $158,000–$173,000 | +$5,000 |

The 401(k) limit applies across 401(k), 403(b), most 457(b) governmental plans, and the federal Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) — if you participate in more than one of these in the same year, your combined elective deferrals across all of them still can't exceed the single $25,500 cap (plus catch-up, if eligible).

## Who Gets the "Super" Catch-Up — and Why It's Different

The $11,750 super catch-up isn't available to everyone 50 and older — it's specifically for workers who turn **60, 61, 62, or 63** at any point during 2027, a provision created by SECURE 2.0. If you're 50–59 or 64+, you still get the standard $8,500 catch-up instead. The super catch-up replaces the standard catch-up rather than stacking on top of it, so the practical math for someone in the 60–63 window is:

- Base deferral: $25,500
- Super catch-up: $11,750
- **Total 2027 max: $37,250**

For everyone else 50+, it's $25,500 + $8,500 = **$34,000** total. If you're not sure how a higher contribution rate affects your take-home pay, the [savings goal calculator](/calculators/savings-goal-calculator) can show you the per-paycheck impact of dialing your deferral percentage up to hit either cap by year-end.

## Why the Increase Is $1,000 Again

401(k) limits are indexed to inflation under IRC Section 415(d), using a formula based on the Consumer Price Index. The 2026 limit also rose by $1,000 (from $23,500 to $24,500), and 2027's identical $1,000 jump reflects continued — though moderating — inflation pressure through 2026. The IRA limit, by contrast, moves in smaller $500 increments and only when cumulative inflation clears certain rounding thresholds, which is why it's held flat at $7,500 for a second straight year while the 401(k) limit keeps climbing.

## How to Actually Use the Extra $1,000

Getting from this year's contribution rate to the new max isn't automatic — your employer won't bump your deferral percentage on their own. A few ways to approach it:

1. **If you're maxing out already:** recalculate your per-paycheck deferral percentage for 2027. $25,500 spread across 26 biweekly paychecks is about $981 per paycheck (slightly less if your plan allows true-up contributions later in the year).
2. **If you're contributing a flat percentage of pay:** check whether that percentage still gets you to $25,500 at your current salary — a raise might mean you're now leaving room on the table, or hitting the cap earlier than you'd like.
3. **If you're not maxing out:** even directing half of the $1,000 increase ($500/year, or about $19 per biweekly paycheck) toward your 401(k) compounds meaningfully over a multi-decade career. The [retirement income calculator](/calculators/retirement-income-calculator) can model how a small increase in today's contribution rate changes your projected retirement withdrawal amount and how long your balance lasts.
4. **If you're 60–63 in 2027:** confirm with your plan administrator that the super catch-up is actually enabled — not every plan automatically supports it, and you may need to opt in separately from your standard catch-up election.

## 401(k) vs. IRA: Why the Gap Keeps Widening

With the 401(k) limit now at $25,500 and the IRA limit frozen at $7,500, the gap between the two has grown to $18,000 — the widest it's been. That matters for anyone deciding where to prioritize savings:

- **If your employer offers a match**, contribute at least enough to capture the full match before thinking about the IRA — that's an immediate, guaranteed return no IRA can match.
- **Beyond the match**, a 401(k)'s higher ceiling makes it the primary vehicle for aggressive savers, while an IRA (especially Roth, if you're income-eligible) adds flexibility — different withdrawal rules, often more investment choices, and in Roth's case, tax-free growth.
- **If you're self-employed or your employer doesn't offer a plan**, the IRA limit is what you're working with, which is one more reason the frozen $7,500 figure stings more for that group than for W-2 employees with access to a 401(k).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the 2027 401(k) contribution limit?**
$25,500 for employee elective deferrals, up $1,000 from the 2026 limit of $24,500. This applies to 401(k), 403(b), most 457(b) plans, and the federal TSP.

**How much can I contribute if I'm 50 or older in 2027?**
$34,000 total ($25,500 base plus an $8,500 catch-up) — unless you're turning 60, 61, 62, or 63 during 2027, in which case your catch-up is $11,750 instead, for a $37,250 total.

**Did the IRA contribution limit also go up for 2027?**
No. The traditional and Roth IRA limit stays at $7,500 for 2027, the same as 2026. The IRA catch-up for age 50+ also stays flat at $1,100.

**Does my 401(k) contribution automatically increase to the new limit?**
No. Most plans require you to actively update your deferral percentage or dollar amount — check your plan's enrollment portal, especially if you contribute a flat dollar amount rather than a percentage of pay.

**When do the new 2027 limits take effect?**
January 1, 2027, for the 2027 plan year. The 2026 limits ($24,500 base, $8,000/$11,250 catch-up) still apply to contributions made during 2026.

**Is the SIMPLE IRA limit changing too?**
Yes — the SIMPLE plan elective deferral limit rises to $17,500 for 2027, up $500 from $17,000 in 2026.

## The Bottom Line

The 2027 401(k) limit of $25,500 — plus up to $11,750 in catch-up room for ages 60–63 — gives most savers more pretax space than ever, but none of it applies automatically. If you're already at the max, recheck your per-paycheck deferral percentage so you land on $25,500 (or $37,250) by December 2027 rather than falling short.
