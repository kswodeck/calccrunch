## When Does the 2027-28 FAFSA Open?

The **2027-28 FAFSA is on track to open to all applicants on October 1, 2026** — the Department of Education's traditional annual launch date, now restored after the delayed and staggered rollouts of the 2024-25 and 2025-26 cycles. The 2027-28 form uses your **2025 federal tax return** (pulled in automatically via direct IRS data transfer for most filers) and covers the academic year that starts in fall 2027. Treat October 1 as the target date — the Department has said it's "on track" to hit it, but as with any federal system launch, it's worth checking [studentaid.gov](https://studentaid.gov) directly as the date approaches in case of last-minute schedule changes.

Filing early matters because many state and school financial-aid programs award funds on a first-come, first-served basis until they run out — waiting until December or January can mean missing out on aid that would have been available in October.

## What's New for the 2027-28 Cycle

A few improvements carry over from recent form updates and should make filing faster this year:

- **Pre-populated data for returning filers.** If you or your family completed a FAFSA before, much of your information carries over, so renewing should take a fraction of the time of your first submission.
- **Shared parent information across siblings.** Parents with more than one child applying for aid can reuse their financial information across each child's form instead of re-entering it from scratch.
- **Direct IRS data transfer** continues to be the fastest way to fill in income and tax information — it also reduces the chance of errors that trigger slow manual verification.

## Key FAFSA Dates to Know

| Date | What Happens |
|---|---|
| Now – Sept 2026 | Beta/early access period for some applicants; early submissions are processed as real filings, not test runs |
| Mid-September 2026 | Waitlist access expands ahead of the general public launch |
| **October 1, 2026** | 2027-28 FAFSA opens to all applicants |
| Ongoing after opening | Many state and school aid programs award funds first-come, first-served — file as early as you can |
| June 30, 2028 | Federal deadline to submit the 2027-28 FAFSA (final cutoff — most aid is long gone well before this date) |

Note that your state and individual colleges almost always set their own priority deadlines that fall much earlier than the federal June 30 cutoff — check each school's financial aid page directly, since these vary widely.

## How to Prepare Before October 1

You don't have to wait for the form to open to get ready. Gather these now so filing takes minutes instead of hours once it's live:

1. **FSA ID.** Both the student and at least one contributing parent (for dependent students) need a verified [Federal Student Aid ID](https://studentaid.gov) — this alone can take a day or two to verify, so set it up early.
2. **2025 tax return information** for the student (and parents, if dependent) — Social Security numbers, income, and tax filing status.
3. **Records of untaxed income and current assets** — bank statements, investment account balances, and records of child support received, if applicable.
4. **A list of colleges you're applying to** — you can list multiple schools on one FAFSA, and each one that receives your form can offer aid based on it.

## FAFSA vs. CSS Profile: Do You Need Both?

The FAFSA determines eligibility for federal aid (grants, work-study, and federal loans) and is required at nearly every U.S. college. Some private colleges and a smaller number of public universities also require the **CSS Profile**, a separate, more detailed application used to award the school's own institutional financial aid. If you're applying to selective private schools, check each one's financial aid page — you may need to submit both forms, often on different timelines, so it's worth building a simple checklist per school rather than assuming one form covers everything.

## Common FAFSA Mistakes to Avoid

- **Waiting to file until you've been accepted everywhere.** You can list schools before hearing back — add or update the school list later if needed.
- **Guessing at tax figures.** Use the IRS direct data transfer option whenever it's available instead of manually entering numbers, to avoid verification delays.
- **Missing school-specific deadlines.** The federal deadline (June 30, 2028) is far later than most school and state priority deadlines — filing "on time" federally can still mean missing out on aid.
- **Not renewing every year.** FAFSA isn't a one-time form — it must be resubmitted for every academic year you want aid for, including the 2027-28 cycle covered here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to file a new FAFSA every year?**
Yes. Financial aid is awarded one academic year at a time, so returning students need to file a new FAFSA (often called a "renewal") each cycle, even if nothing about their finances has changed.

**What tax year does the 2027-28 FAFSA use?**
It uses your **2025** federal tax return — FAFSA always uses tax information from two years prior to the academic year it covers.

**Can I submit the FAFSA before October 1?**
Some applicants may have early or beta access before the general public launch, and those submissions are processed as real filings — you do not need to file again once the form fully opens. If you don't have early access, October 1 is your target date.

**Does filing early guarantee more aid?**
Filing early doesn't change the *formula* used to calculate your aid eligibility, but it matters because many state grants, school scholarships, and work-study funds are limited and awarded on a first-come, first-served basis. Filing late can mean those pools are already exhausted even if you'd otherwise have qualified.
