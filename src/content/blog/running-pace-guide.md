## What Is Running Pace and How Do You Calculate It?

Your running pace is how long it takes you to cover one mile or one kilometer, and the formula is simple: **Pace = Time ÷ Distance**. If you run 5 miles in 40 minutes, your pace is 40 ÷ 5 = 8 minutes per mile (written as 8:00/mi). Once you know your pace, you can flip the formula to solve for time (Time = Pace × Distance) or distance (Distance = Time ÷ Pace) — which is exactly what our [pace calculator](/calculators/pace-calculator) does automatically when you enter any two of the three values.

Pace is different from speed. Speed (mph or km/h) tells you how far you travel in an hour — higher is faster. Pace (minutes per mile or km) tells you how long a mile or km takes — lower is faster. Most runners think in pace because it maps directly onto race distances, while treadmills usually display speed. To convert between them, divide 60 by your pace in minutes: a 10:00/mile pace is 60 ÷ 10 = 6.0 mph.

## Step-by-Step: Finding Your Pace

1. **Time yourself** over a known distance — a measured mile, a GPS watch run, or a race with an official clock.
2. **Convert your time to seconds** for easier math: minutes × 60 + seconds (add hours × 3,600 for longer runs).
3. **Divide by the distance** in miles or kilometers to get seconds per unit.
4. **Convert back to minutes:seconds** by dividing by 60 — the whole number is minutes, the remainder is seconds.

**Worked example**: You run a 5K (3.10686 miles) in 28 minutes 30 seconds (1,710 seconds).
Pace = 1,710 ÷ 3.10686 ≈ 550.4 seconds/mile ≈ **9:10 per mile**.
In kilometers: 1,710 ÷ 5 = 342 seconds/km = **5:42 per km**.

## Converting Between Minutes-Per-Mile and Minutes-Per-Km

Since 1 mile = 1.609344 km, converting a mile pace to a km pace means dividing by 1.609344 (and multiplying to go the other direction):

- **Mile pace → km pace**: divide the pace (in seconds) by 1.609344
- **Km pace → mile pace**: multiply the pace (in seconds) by 1.609344

## How Pace Relates to Calories Burned

Pace doesn't just tell you your finish time — it's also one of the biggest drivers of how many calories a run burns, since faster paces raise your MET (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) value. A 9.8 MET effort (roughly 6 mph, or a 10:00/mile pace) burns meaningfully more per minute than an easy jog. ## Tips for Improving Your Pace

- **Train at multiple paces.** Easy runs build aerobic base; tempo runs and intervals raise your threshold pace over time.
- **Negative-split practice runs.** Start slightly slower than goal pace and speed up in the second half — it's a common race-day strategy worth rehearsing in training.
- **Track trends, not single runs.** Pace varies with heat, hills, and fatigue. Look at your average pace over weeks, not one bad (or great) run.
- **Fuel and hydrate.** Under-fueling or dehydration slows pace well before it feels like a problem — pair your training with the [calories burned calculator](/calculators/calories-burned-calculator) to make sure you're eating enough to support your mileage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I calculate my running pace?**
Divide your total time by your total distance: Pace = Time ÷ Distance. Running 5 miles in 40 minutes gives a pace of 40 ÷ 5 = 8:00 per mile.

**What pace do I need for a sub-4-hour marathon?**
About 9:09 per mile (5:41 per km) held for all 26.2188 miles: 4 hours ÷ 26.2188 miles ≈ 9.15 minutes per mile.

**Is race pace faster or slower than training pace?**
For a marathon, most runners end up a little slower than easy training pace once fatigue accumulates over the full distance. For shorter races like a 5K, race pace is typically faster than easy pace because the effort level is much higher.

**How do I convert pace to speed?**
Divide 60 by your pace in minutes. A 10:00/mile pace equals 60 ÷ 10 = 6.0 mph. To convert speed back to pace, divide 60 by the speed instead.

**Does a faster pace always burn more calories?**
Generally yes, per minute — but total calories depend on both pace and duration. A longer run at an easy pace can burn more total calories than a short run at a fast pace. Use the [calories burned calculator](/calculators/calories-burned-calculator) to compare specific scenarios.
