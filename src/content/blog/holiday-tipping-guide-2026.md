Most people figure out who to tip for the holidays by guessing — and either over-tip out of guilt or under-tip because they genuinely don't know the going rate. The short answer: for anyone who provides you a recurring service (a weekly house cleaner, your regular hairdresser, a daily dog walker), a holiday tip equal to the cost of one typical visit is the standard baseline. For one-time or occasional help, a smaller flat amount — usually $10 to $50 — is appropriate. The full breakdown by service provider is below, along with how to fit the total into your existing holiday budget instead of treating it as a surprise expense.

## The General Rule of Thumb

Holiday tipping guidelines boil down to one simple principle: **the tip scales with how often you see the person and how personal the relationship is.**

- **Weekly or biweekly service** (house cleaner, nanny, regular hairdresser): tip the equivalent of **one full visit's cost**, handed over at your last appointment before the holidays.
- **Daily, live-in, or highly personal care** (full-time nanny, daily dog walker, in-home caregiver): tip closer to **one week's pay**.
- **Occasional or seasonal help** (snow removal crew, holiday décor installer): a smaller flat amount, often **$20-$50 per person**, is appropriate — you don't owe them a full week's pay for a few visits a year.
- **One-time service providers** (a contractor who did a single job, a plumber who fixed a leak) generally **don't need a holiday tip at all** — you already paid for that job in full when it was done.

## Dining Out During the Holidays

If your tipping question is really about restaurants and delivery rather than service providers, the rule shifts slightly for the holiday season itself. Because the people working on Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, or New Year's are often giving up time with their own families, it's common practice to tip a bit above your usual range:

- **Restaurant dining**: 22-25% instead of the standard 18-20%, especially for a holiday meal out.
- **Food delivery**: add an extra $2-$5 on top of your normal tip for deliveries during peak holiday hours or bad weather.
- **Hotel staff** (if traveling): lean toward the higher end of the usual range for housekeeping and bellhop tips.

## How to Budget for Holiday Tipping Without the Stress

Holiday tipping rarely gets its own line item — it quietly piggybacks on an already-stretched December budget, which is exactly why it feels painful. Treating it as a planned expense instead of an afterthought fixes that:

1. **List everyone you plan to tip**, using the chart above as a starting point.
2. **Estimate a total dollar range** rather than an exact number — most households land somewhere between $100 and $400 depending on how many recurring service providers they use.
3. **Add it to your overall holiday spending plan.** If you're already using the [holiday gift budget calculator](/calculators/holiday-gift-budget-calculator) to plan gifts, add your tipping total as its own category rather than letting it eat into gift money unexpectedly.
4. **Check it against your regular monthly budget** with the [budget calculator](/calculators/budget-calculator) to make sure a one-time December expense doesn't crowd out rent, bills, or debt payments.

Spreading a $200 tipping budget across even four or five weeks of December — roughly $40-$50 a week — is far less stressful than discovering you need it all at once in the week before Christmas.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is holiday tipping mandatory?**
No. It's a customary gesture of appreciation, not an obligation with a fixed legal or contractual amount. If your budget is tight this year, a smaller, genuine tip (or a thank-you note instead of cash for occasional help) is completely acceptable.

**Should I tip in cash or with a check/gift card?**
Cash is the traditional and most-appreciated option for most service providers since it's immediately usable and doesn't carry fees. For USPS mail carriers specifically, federal rules cap cash and cash-equivalent gifts at $20, though a small non-cash gift is allowed on top of that.

**Do I need to tip every person on the chart above?**
No — the chart is a menu, not a checklist. Tip the people you interact with regularly or who meaningfully help your household, and skip or scale down categories that don't apply to your situation.

**What if I can't afford to tip as much as the guidelines suggest?**
Scale your list, not just your amounts. It's generally better to give a smaller, genuine tip to your top 3-5 most important service providers than to spread a token amount across a 15-person list you can't actually afford.

**When should I give holiday tips?**
Most people give them at the last service visit before the holidays — typically mid-to-late December. For once-a-year providers like a snow removal crew, after the first significant service of the season also works.
